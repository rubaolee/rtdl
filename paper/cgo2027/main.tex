\begin{abstract}
Ray-tracing APIs validate program and platform legality but leave application
meaning distributed across host composition, multiple device callbacks,
payload and attribute conventions, continuation code, and the executable that
is ultimately launched. We present \rtdl, a bounded whole-protocol compilation
and admission model for repurposed OptiX computations. A backend-neutral,
role-indexed Callback IR represents typed callback computation and effects;
fixed-constructor semantic, physical, continuation, and identity authorities extend
that IR into one admission unit. \rtdl checks cross-role effects, semantic ABI
ownership, physical bindings, status-gated continuation and completeness, and
executable identity. Its public GPU surface has two fixed protocol constructors
over two physical geometry kinds and one bounded user-authored built-in-triangle
Callback-IR template; arbitrary IR and new protocol topologies remain unsupported.

On two project-designed non-rendering tasks, single-leaf mutations holding
compiler projection bytes fixed show that all 19 populated contract leaves are
decision-bearing: 7 role/effect, 1 semantic-ABI, 7 physical-binding,
2 continuation/completeness, and 2 executable-identity leaves. This establishes
liveness for these two contracts, not vocabulary completeness, target-projection
correctness, or a soundness theorem. In a disclosed two-line repaired PyOptiX
FFI route with OptiX~9 typed-payload semantics active, all five preregistered
fault realizations reach launch; a separate pinned NVIDIA \owl route also
launches all five. The supported responsibility result is three full wrong-output
residuals, one partial status-gated continuation/completeness residual, and
checked-executable binding support; \rtdl rejects the corresponding
declarations before launch.

Across nine project-authored ports of published RT mappings,
13 selected lanes execute exactly through the base callback path and six
application-neutral composition batches. One project-selected SQL bag equijoin
reuses an existing fixed constructor without a core change, but prospective unbiased
new-application exams, third-party authors, and human usability studies remain
zero. For performance, we compare current \rtdl with Direct CUDA/OptiX and
\pyoptixcompat, a pinned NVIDIA \texttt{otk-pyoptix}~9.1.0 source build using
the OptiX~9.0 compatibility API. On two matched tasks on one RTX~4000 Ada,
steady RTDL/\pyoptixcompat passes the registered 1.05 noninferiority gate,
with median differences of $-17\,\mu$s and $+1.22\,\mu$s, while post-import
deployment-cold and prepare show setup deltas of 162--223\,ms. The Direct arm
is descriptive. \rtdl does not discover ray-tracing mappings, execute arbitrary
Callback IR, prove accepted-program correctness, or establish usability
superiority or universal performance parity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Hardware ray-tracing APIs are increasingly repurposed for non-rendering
computations such as spatial queries, graph processing, databases, and
simulation. A recent review identifies 59 papers and systematically analyzes
35 proposed RT solutions spanning 32 distinct problems, while also observing
that profitable mappings remain irregular and difficult to
predict~\cite{meneses2026rtcoresurvey}.

For the application-to-RT correspondence studied here, we organize relevant
systems by three established responsibilities.
\emph{Interface systems} validate shader-stage interfaces, access contracts,
and target legality~\cite{parker2010optix,nvidiaOptix9Guide,microsoftDXRPAQ,
khronosVulkanInterfaces,slangCapabilities}. \emph{Construction systems}
expose or organize host bindings, shader modules, acceleration structures,
pipelines, shader binding tables (SBTs), resources, and
launches~\cite{nvidiaPyOptix,nvidiaOWL,he2017shaderComponents,he2018slang}.
\emph{Mapping systems} trace, specialize, or translate higher-level
computations to RT-capable backends
~\cite{jakob2022drjit,zheng2022luisa,frolov2024crossrt}. Systems may span
classes; these labels identify their primary responsibility rather than their
implementation form.

Even when one system spans these responsibilities, the documented checking
units above do not jointly admit a repurposed computation as one
application-semantic protocol. Its
meaning is distributed across multiple device roles, payload and attribute
slots, geometry construction, pipeline and SBT bindings, device status, host
continuation, and the executable bytes that finally launch. Each piece may be
legal while the complete protocol is wrong. A hit program can
interpret an attribute as a primitive index although the intersection program
wrote an application item identifier; a callback can omit state required by a
downstream role; a host can consume a capacity-truncated output despite an
overflow status; or a reviewed declaration can be paired with a different
executable. Such faults are particularly dangerous in non-rendering workloads,
where application-level exactness and completeness invariants are not
represented by the RT API contract.

This paper studies a fourth responsibility, \emph{protocol admission}, and
asks a deliberately narrower question than automatic mapping:
\emph{after a developer has chosen a traversal formulation, can the complete
callback protocol become the unit of compilation and admission?} We present
\rtdl, which implements two fixed protocol constructors over two physical
geometry kinds plus one bounded user-authored built-in-triangle template. The source and
physical plan are checked as one obligation set; an accepted program is bound
to generated code, target and native identities; and execution is exposed
through a typed materialize--prepare--execute--close lifecycle. The application
continues to own its algorithm, semantic oracle, and the decision to use ray
tracing.

This bounded framing is important. \rtdl is not OptiX-equivalent, does not
execute arbitrary verified Callback IR on the public GPU path, and does not
establish prospective or third-party generalization. Its value proposition is
instead analogous to a protocol IDL: it does not simplify the application
algorithm, but it makes selected cross-component mismatches unrepresentable or
rejectable before launch.

This paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a backend-neutral Callback IR with typed records and views, bounded
  control flow, role-indexed effects, numeric and resource contracts,
  deterministic identity, and a fail-closed verifier;
  \item a protocol-admission compilation unit, distinct from interface
  checking, construction, and mapping, that combines the verified IR with
  separate authorities for fixed-constructor semantics, physical bindings, continuation,
  and identity to check
  cross-role effects, semantic ABI ownership, physical bindings,
  status-gated continuation and completeness, and checked-executable identity as
  one unit, and carries acceptance through a typed
  materialize--prepare--execute--close lifecycle;
  \item a bounded OptiX implementation with two fixed public GPU protocol
  constructors and one user-authored template inside a fixed static-triangle
  \texttt{u32x3} physical/ABI shape, exercised by nine project-authored ports over thirteen
  selected paper-derived lanes and six application-neutral composition
  batches. These routes instantiate two of six OptiX~9 build-input enum kinds
  (two of four leaf kinds). The counts report kind presence rather than
  complete category support. The routes do not form an arbitrary
  Callback-IR-to-GPU compiler; and
  \item a claim-calibrated evaluation: all 19 populated leaves in two contracts
  are decision-bearing, with seven, one, seven, two, and two leaves for
  CP001--CP005, respectively; active
  OptiX typed-payload and pinned \owl executions support three full
  wrong-output residuals, one partial status-gated continuation/completeness
  residual, and
  executable-binding support; one project-selected reuse and one failed
  transfer are preserved; and a same-target three-arm successor reports both
  the passing steady and failing setup results against \pyoptixcompat, with
  Direct CUDA/OptiX descriptive.
\end{itemize}

The leaf sweep is liveness evidence for two populated contracts. It does not
establish vocabulary completeness, target-projection correctness, semantic
soundness, or correctness of arbitrary accepted applications.

\section{Motivating Failure: Asking ``Which Item?'' and Getting ``Which Shelf?''}

\noindent\textbf{One-sentence version.} \rtdl prevents a repurposed RT program
from silently returning \emph{where an object is stored} when the application
asked \emph{which object it is}.

Imagine a warehouse with shelf 0 holding item 10 and shelf 1 holding item 20.
Lookup 100 should return item 10, and lookup 101 should return item 20. A buggy
worker instead returns the shelf numbers 0 and 1. Nothing crashes: 0, 1, 10,
and 20 are all valid integers. The answers have the right machine type but the
wrong meaning. Figure~\ref{fig:semantic-abi-example} shows this exact failure.

The executed RT program has exactly this shape. Primitive positions 0 and 1 are
the shelves; application object identifiers 10 and 20 are the item labels. The
intersection callback produces attribute slot zero and any-hit consumes it to
emit a pair. The defective producer writes the primitive position rather than
the application identifier. The slot is still a valid \texttt{u32}; native
access validation, GAS, program groups, pipeline, SBT, and launch all remain
legal. The evaluated PyOptiX, active typed-OptiX, and OWL routes therefore reach
launch and return $(100,0)$ and $(101,1)$. \rtdl instead compares the promised
meaning with the regenerated callback and rejects before launch.

The error is not a bad number; it is a broken agreement about what the number
means. We call that agreement a cross-role semantic ABI. Checking it requires
relating the producer, consumer, physical binding, and launched executable.
The same whole-program view is needed for effects, capacity status, and
continuation.

The failure does not depend on spatial expertise. In a database join it is the
difference between a row identifier and a storage position; in sorting it is
the difference between a persistent record identifier and its current array
position. We use the spatial instance because it is the actual executed
counterexample. Sorting is intuition only: \rtdl neither discovers a sorting
mapping nor proves a comparator, rank computation, or scatter correct.

An application test catches this particular input because item IDs 10 and 20
differ from shelf positions 0 and 1. If the numbers happened to coincide, that
test could hide the bug. More generally, tests sample inputs; they do not reject
the broken agreement itself. \rtdl therefore complements rather than replaces
testing: the oracle checks observed answers, while admission checks the declared
protocol before any answer is produced.


\section{Callback Protocol IR}
\label{sec:callback-ir}

\rtdl does not treat callback entry points as unrelated shader functions. It
first lowers a complete callback unit to a backend-neutral, typed
\emph{Callback IR}. The IR is the semantic nucleus of protocol admission: it
represents callback computation, role topology, machine-visible records, and
role-indexed effects without embedding Python, CUDA, PTX, or OptiX source
syntax. It is not, however, another name for the entire protocol. Nominal task
meaning, physical bindings, host continuation, and executable identity enter
through separate authorities described below.

\subsection{Program model}

Restricted Python arrives as UTF-8 text. An AST allowlist parses it; the module
is never imported or executed for discovery. The frontend resolves only
declared records, frozen constants, same-unit bounded helpers, and selected
intrinsics, then emits the forms summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:callback-ir-model}. Serialized IR is not trusted. Reading its
canonical JSON reconstructs typed objects and reruns the complete verifier.

A role cannot call a raw traversal intrinsic. Instead it returns exactly one
typed effect that the trusted wrapper realizes, as shown in

This distinction matters because a language role need not correspond one-to-one

hit role. For the built-in-triangle topology, bounds and intersection are
forbidden because the hardware owns them, while ray construction, miss,
finalization, and at least one hit role remain. Every required role has exactly
one function. Supplying an \texttt{any\_hit} role also requires an explicit
delivery contract; the existence of that enum does not prove confluence or
order independence for arbitrary logic.

\subsection{Fail-closed verification}

The following judgments summarize implemented checks; they are explanatory
notation, not a mechanized soundness theorem. Expression checking derives
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma \vdash e : \tau .
\end{equation}
Statement checking returns a typed environment, whether every path has
returned, a conservative static-iteration count, and the effects observed:
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma;r \vdash s
  \Rightarrow \langle \Gamma',q,n,\epsilon\rangle .
\end{equation}
Names are single-assignment except for type-preserving mutation inside static
loops. Both sides of a non-returning branch must define the same typed names;
loop locals cannot escape; and every role/helper path must return. For each
role $r$, the verifier enforces its exact signature and
\begin{equation}
  M \vdash F_r : \epsilon
  \quad\text{where}\quad
  \epsilon \subseteq \mathsf{AllowedEffects}(r).
\end{equation}
Whole-program checking then verifies schema and source identity, record and
helper acyclicity, numeric policy, payload/attribute slot counts, role
cardinality, control-flow and call-depth budgets, production linkage, and any
required external geometry authority:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{G};\mathcal{R} \vdash Q
  \Downarrow
  \mathsf{VerifiedCallbackProgram}(h_{\mathrm{IR}},h_{\mathrm{effects}}).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{G}$ is an out-of-program geometry authority and $\mathcal{R}$ is
the compiler-owned role topology. A manifest may name a verified geometry
contract but cannot mint its authority. The result is typed, bounded,
role/effect-closed IR with deterministic identity; it is not physical execution
authority and does not prove application-level correctness.

The same verified IR also has a deterministic CPU interpretation with explicit
runtime status. This supplies a reference semantics for the restricted
computation. It does not establish GPU lowering correctness by itself: the
target path is separately regenerated and compared through the layered
admission mechanism below.

\section{Whole-Protocol Admission}

\subsection{Compilation unit after IR verification}

Whole-protocol admission begins after Callback IR verification. ABI compilation
re-verifies the IR and derives deterministic role inputs, effect variants,
symbols, and status codes. The fixed-constructor specification adds nominal task
and slot meaning; the physical authority adds geometry and storage bindings;
and the lifecycle adds continuation and executable identity. Admission compares
these layered facts as one compilation unit before any GPU state exists.

The public GPU interface is narrower than that frontend. On the fixed route, a
caller selects one of two protocol specifications, supplies its parameters,
the sole standard physical plan, an external proof identity, and
constructor-specific inputs; the compiler re-derives its source and manifest.
On the bounded authoring route, the caller instead supplies restricted source,
a manifest, names and expressions, while the physical/ABI template remains
fixed. Other verified source remains available for verification and CPU
interpretation but has no general GPU lowering authority.

\subsection{Five admission obligations}

\paragraph{Cross-role effects.}
The IR verifier restricts every role to a closed set of typed returned effects.
The fixed-constructor declaration records the role/effect topology it requires,
while the target projection re-derives effect variants from the compiled ABI.
Admission rejects an omitted required effect or an effect outside that topology.

\paragraph{Semantic ABI ownership.}
The generic IR records machine payload/attribute types but does not make a
\texttt{u32} denote an application item identifier. That nominal meaning comes
from the fixed-constructor contract and is compared with the compiled row source.
The evaluated CP002 leaf is attribute slot zero; nominal payload ownership is
not demonstrated. Matching bit widths are therefore insufficient.

\paragraph{Physical binding.}
Callback assumptions are checked against the typed custom-AABB or built-in
triangle physical plan, including field-to-geometry bindings. This prevents a
coherently typed but semantically permuted geometry layout.

\paragraph{Status-gated continuation and completeness.}
IR/ABI status vocabulary, the fixed-constructor completeness contract, and lifecycle
sequencing jointly form this obligation. Host continuation must observe status,
and a failed or truncated execution cannot expose an application result. Merely
reading status before copying output is insufficient when a non-OK capacity
condition does not suppress that output.

\paragraph{Checked executable identity.}
In the current materializer, the two CP005 fields carry the same selected
executable digest. Their comparison is identity-binding instrumentation, not
independent extraction. The later content-addressed deployment and execution
checks rehash source/PTX, proof and physical authorities, target, provider, and
native identities to establish the executable chain of custody.

These categories are related rather than five independent theorems. Effect
closure and status are hierarchical, while semantic ABI and physical binding
form a shared schema layer. Executable identity carries the other decisions to
the launched bytes and is treated as identity-binding support, not as a fifth
independent novelty count.

\subsection{Public lifecycle and scope}

The stable public GPU surface exposes two compiler-owned fixed protocol
constructors:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a custom-AABB bounded relation that emits canonical pairs with an
  explicit capacity; and
  \item a built-in-triangle all-hit computation followed by a checked unsigned
  64-bit reducer.
\end{enumerate}

The same stable module also exposes one bounded, caller-authored triangle
template. Its fixed shape is one static GAS, a three-\texttt{u32} payload/output
record, and two \texttt{u32} metadata views. It is not a third fixed constructor
and adds no physical geometry kind. A public Particle strict-interior frontdoor
is an application specialization of this capability, not another general
template.

Materialization creates no GPU state. It re-derives a fixed constructor's
callback or compiles the bounded template's source, compares projections, and
binds the result to a target. Preparation creates an owner local to one process
and thread. Execution returns output only after status and identity checks;
close destroys the owner. PTX, entry points, program groups, SBT records,
pipelines, and native handles are never user inputs.

The implementation supports trace depth one and callable depth zero. The
public surface excludes arbitrary recursion, dynamic dispatch, raw pointers,
arbitrary atomics, nested \texttt{optixTrace}, user callables, curves, spheres,
motion, instancing, micromaps, arbitrary multi-report intersections, new
geometry families, and new role or continuation topologies. These are
capability limits, not future features implicitly credited to the current
system.

\subsection{Terminology, denominator, and protocol-shape algebra}

We separate five quantities that a bare word ``family'' would conflate. A
\emph{build-input kind} is one member of OptiX's platform enum. A
\emph{leaf-primitive kind} is a geometry-bearing member rather than an
instance-graph input. A \emph{physical geometry kind} is one geometry choice
implemented by RTDL. A \emph{fixed protocol constructor} is a closed public
factory with a prescribed role/result contract. An \emph{application-semantic
protocol shape} is a structural callback protocol independent of application
or dataset names. The pinned OptiX~9 header has six build-input kinds---triangle,
custom primitive, instance, instance pointer, curve and sphere---of which four
are leaf primitives. Current public physical-schema routes instantiate triangle
and custom primitive: kind-presence for $2/6$ build-input kinds and $2/4$
leaf-primitive kinds. This is not feature-complete support for every
configuration within either kind. Its constructor enum has two members, its
bounded user-source template count is one, and the open set of
application-semantic protocol shapes has no finite coverage denominator.

For future extension tests we freeze a structural shape as
$S=\langle G,R,V,E,H,B,C,X,L\rangle$: build/scene graph $G$; callback-role
topology $R$; typed views $V$ and role effects $E$; nominal hit-channel
producer, consumer and ownership relations $H$; buffer/GAS/IAS/SBT bindings
$B$; continuation, status and result-commit automaton $C$; identity bind-set
$X$; and numeric/resource limits $L$. Two shapes are equal only when their
canonical structural forms are byte-equal after alpha-renaming local binders
and sorting only order-insensitive sets. Role arguments, result operators,
graph edges and continuation order remain significant. Application
names, datasets and record spelling do not define a shape. Nominal semantic
values, verified IR/proof bindings and instance parameters define a separate
protocol instance; target, provider and executable identities define a
deployment. An unexpressible protocol is \textsc{unmapped}, not coerced to the
nearest current constructor. This algebra freezes a falsifiable target for a
later family-schema compiler; the current implementation is not such a
compiler, and prospective frozen-core extension exams remain zero.

The two physical kinds were author-selected to span different hit-production
boundaries: built-in hits produced by OptiX and custom-primitive hits produced
by a callback, while covering the authored cohort. This is a useful contrast,
not a representative or prospective sample of the open protocol-shape universe.

\subsection{Admission judgment}

During materialization, the implementation constructs two internal
projections. The declaration-side projection
$D=\langle f,t,E,A,B,C,X\rangle$ contains fixed-constructor identity $f$, task-semantics
identity $t$, IR-side role/effect map $E$, evaluated attribute-slot ownership map $A$,
visible physical binding map $B$, continuation policy $C$, and the executable
identity $X$ selected during materialization. The target-side projection $P$
contains corresponding primed fields plus generated device source $S_d$ and
host materializer $S_h$. Its CP001--CP004 facts are separately re-derived from
the regenerated callback ABI, physical authority, and compiled contract. For
CP005, both projections receive the same materializer-selected executable
digest. Maps are sorted and all structures use canonical JSON serialization
before hashing. Neither projection is an application-authored certificate.

The admission relation is deliberately simple and auditable:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathsf{admit}(D,P) \iff{}&
  (f,t,B)=(f',t',B') \land E=E' \land A=A' \\
 &{}\land C=C' \land X=X'.
\end{split}
\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
Malformed or incorrectly sealed inputs fail before this relation. A failed
conjunct emits a stable reason identifier (CP001--CP005) and no executable
capability. Family and task identity are included in the physical-binding
conjunct: changing the semantic domain cannot masquerade as a sixth generic
mechanism.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the important boundary. The semantic and
physical facts in $D$ are derived from the verified constructor/template specification,
compiler-owned IR-side effects, and visible physical plan. Their counterparts
in $P$ come from the regenerated target ABI and compiler-owned physical
objects, not by serializing $D$ again. For CP001--CP004 these are distinct
derivation paths inside one implementation, not independent implementations.
Their agreement
establishes consistency among represented facts under the trusted computing
base; it does not prove target-projection correctness or that the application
computes the right function. CP005 leaf mutation establishes comparator
sensitivity only; the later content-addressed and runtime checks provide its
chain-of-custody evidence.

Acceptance records the identities of the program, target, physical schema,
contract, ABI, executable, PTX, and native library. Execution rechecks the PTX
and native-library identities, validates device status before returning output,
verifies the output digest, and binds a traversal receipt to the expected route.
Executable identity is therefore a chain from admission to observation;
Eq.~\ref{eq:admission} alone is not a functional correctness proof.

\subsection{What the worked mismatch establishes}

The rows in Figure~\ref{fig:semantic-abi-example} come from the preserved
executions rather than a paper-only illustration. The public \rtdl lifecycle
returns $\{(100,10),(101,20)\}$ through real OptiX traversal. The corresponding
defective callback is legal OptiX, and the evaluated PyOptiX, active typed-OptiX,
and OWL routes all reach launch; the fault returns primitive-index rows
$\{(100,0),(101,1)\}$. Replacing only the nominal contract leaf from
application-item-ID to primitive-index preserves the machine representation and
target projection bytes, yet \rtdl changes \textsc{accept} to the sole CP002
rejection because $A\ne A'$.

This establishes a narrower but useful fact: nominal protocol admission can
make this declared mismatch impossible to launch even though conventional
machine typing and composition accept it. It does not prove that the overlap
mapping is the right algorithm, that the declared identifier is truthful, or
that an accepted result matches an application oracle. Those remain application
obligations.

\section{Implementation and Application Cohort}

The implementation contains the base callback path and six M1--M6
application-neutral composition batches: checked triangle reductions, bounded
relation emission, prepared multi-round spatial composition, exact
predicate/global witness composition, grouped event reduction, and bounded
hierarchy-frontier continuation. Selection depends on typed semantics,
resource/proof inputs, and physical identity rather than application names.

\paragraph{Architecture and trust boundary.}
The parser, typed Callback IR, canonical ABI, role-leaf generator, PTX
composer, projection schemas, exact CP001--CP005 comparator, content addressing,
and lifecycle state discipline serve both fixed constructors; bounded triangle
authoring also reuses this IR/lowering core.
Constructor/template source policy, proof policy, semantic/physical projectors,
wrapper and entry symbols, target compiler, native program bundle, GAS/SBT
layout, launch path, status reduction, and input/output schemas remain
route-specific. ``Shared'' means reuse across current routes, not parametricity
or soundness for an unimplemented protocol shape.

An accepted materialized object can be frozen as a content-addressed
\texttt{.rtdlexe}. The artifact carries the checked projections and exact
composed PTX but grants no deployment authority by itself. An installed trust
root, current signed head, and signed package authorize one exact slot. On a
cache hit the loader rechecks protocol, target, provider, native-producer, and
deployment identities without importing the frontend, code generator, Numba,
or NVRTC; \texttt{prepare} still constructs process-local OptiX objects. The
trust layer does not resist an administrator rolling both installed trust files
back to an older valid signed pair.

The trusted computing base includes the parser and IR verifier, current-route
providers and physical projectors, ABI compiler, code generator and composer,
both projection producers and their comparator, deployment builder/loader and
administrator-installed trust state, exact native producer, CUDA/OptiX driver,
and GPU. The Python interpreter and standard library, OS filesystem and dynamic
loader, process isolation, signing-key custody, deployment administrator, and
Python code already controlling the loader process are also trusted. External
proof objects are checked for form and exact program binding; their truth is an
assumption, and \rtdl does not prove the theorem denoted by an opaque digest.

Adding a physical kind or role/continuation topology currently changes shared
core and native backend; it is not user-level registration. It
requires a protocol type and plan, source/proof policy, both projectors, target
compiler and wrapper, executable record, lifecycle dispatch, deployment and
runtime projections, native OptiX construction, and tests. A new callback
inside the bounded triangle template does not require those changes; that is
template reuse, not evidence that a new protocol shape can be authorized
without modifying \rtdl.

\section{Evaluation}

We ask five questions: (1) are populated declaration leaves decision-bearing;
(2) do OptiX's typed-payload semantics or \owl already prevent the same
faults; (3) what capability is actually executable; (4) can an application
outside the original fixture reuse a frozen fixed constructor without a core change; and
(5) what lifecycle-separated cost remains relative to \pyoptixcompat?

\subsection{Experimental discipline}

Mechanism experiments use two matched non-rendering tasks, nearby legal
controls, single-mechanism mutations, frozen expected outputs, and exact
oracles. The performance experiment uses identical task semantics, input,
precision, output materialization, status-before-output ordering, launch and
synchronization meaning, and paired-block statistics. Ratios are
RTDL/\pyoptixcompat,
so lower is better. Steady execution has a registered 1.05 noninferiority
margin; preparation and post-import deployment cold use 1.10. Preparation is
reported separately and is never treated as free. A gated row passes only when
both its point estimate and the upper endpoint of its 95\% interval do not
exceed the margin. Within every task/regime, the 18 blocks contain each of the
six arm orders three times. Worker medians are integer nanoseconds; half-ns
midpoints from even steady samples are truncated toward zero before ratios.

The reported 18-block study is a registered successor experiment, not the
first outcome-blind performance test. Two relevant predecessor results were
known before its freeze: a 24-block three-arm RTX~A4500 study whose six
RTDL-versus-PyOptiX-compat gates all failed, and a 16-block two-arm RTX~4000 Ada study whose
two steady gates passed and four setup gates failed. An earlier asymmetric
benchmark was also withdrawn after the baseline's avoidable Python work exceeded
its apparent advantage; none of its timings or claims is reused. The successor
fixed its arms, tasks, regimes, thresholds, complete schedule, estimator, and
unconditional acceptance rule before its own timings. It reran all three arms
in 324 fresh processes, reused no predecessor worker or timing, and accepted
every outcome unconditionally.

\subsection{Every populated contract leaf affects admission}

The two accepted task contracts contain 19 populated semantic leaves. We
mutate each leaf alone while holding compiler projection bytes unchanged. All
19 mutations change \textsc{accept} to \textsc{reject}; each produces exactly
one expected finding, and no leaf is classified as non-decision-bearing. This
includes the safety-critical \texttt{finalize: require\_status\_ok} leaf. A
standard-library verifier that imports neither \rtdl nor \pyoptix checks and
recounts the preserved 19-row projection, including the recorded mutations,
decisions, and reason identifiers.

This result establishes liveness only for populated leaves in these two
contracts. It does not establish completeness of the obligation vocabulary or
semantic correctness of arbitrary accepted applications.

\subsection{Residual under active typed OptiX and OWL}

OptiX~9 exposes an important native mechanism that must receive credit:
\texttt{OptixPayloadType} assigns read/write semantics per 32-bit payload word
and shader stage~\cite{nvidiaOptix9Guide}. In a separate strongest-native
configuration, we enabled that mechanism, selected the type at both trace
sites and every program group, and enabled validation \texttt{ALL}. A
source/PTX-identical negative that removed any-hit rights was rejected during
module creation, establishing that the mechanism was active. Nevertheless,
the preregistered typed-route realizations of all five counterexample mechanisms
built, linked, launched, emitted no validation error or
fatal message, and reproduced their registered consequences. Thus the result
is not an experiment against an omitted native facility.

The CP002 path is particularly sharp: intersection reports either an
application item identifier or a primitive index in attribute slot zero;
any-hit reads that slot and writes the relation row directly. No payload access
occurs on this defective path. The examined OptiX headers declare the number
of attribute values and numbered attribute accessors, but expose no per-slot
nominal-meaning declaration. This is a scoped observation about the inspected
OptiX~9 headers, not a claim about every NVIDIA interface.

A pinned NVIDIA \owl arm with validation \texttt{ALL} enabled by a
diagnostic-only overlay separately receives full credit for building acceleration structures,
programs, pipeline, SBT, and launch, and executes its five preregistered invalid
protocol realizations.
The overlay is the sole upstream modification and changes none of those
objects, the launch, or the output. This is functional GTX1070/Pascal evidence,
not RT-core or performance evidence. The examined public API at the pinned OWL revision
exposes payload and attribute counts but not the native payload-type
declaration; obtaining that facility requires leaving the examined abstraction
or modifying its implementation. Table~\ref{tab:residual} reports the OWL
result per mechanism rather than treating five categories as five independent
novelties.

\subsection{One constructive reuse and one preserved failure}

A SQLite integer bag equijoin over a bounded non-null integer domain maps each
key $k$ to the exactly representable binary32 AABB $[k,0,k+1,1]$. Equal keys
overlap with area one and unequal integer keys with area at most zero; row
identifiers preserve duplicate Cartesian multiplicity. The application reuses
the frozen bounded-relation constructor, matches independent SQLite and Python
oracles, and requires no further core, native, physical-geometry, or
fixed-constructor change.

For a database reader, the CP002 distinction is exactly ``row identifier versus
physical AABB position.'' We did not rerun the five invalid-control executions
on the SQL input, so this is evidence of existing-constructor semantic reuse, not a
second residual experiment.

This task was selected by the project after a BED interval-intersection
transfer exposed a missing public overflow mapping and required a core repair.
The SQL result is consequently a constructive, post-failure reuse of a fixed
constructor, not a blind or prospective exam. We preserve both outcomes and
report a prospective unbiased new-application exam count of zero.

The result supports a lifecycle-specific statement. On these two tasks, the
steady median arm differences are $-17\,\mu$s and approximately
$+1.22\,\mu$s; both pass the registered 1.05 noninferiority gate. This is not
evidence of equality or zero execution cost. The observed cold-deployment
(after imports) and prepare setup differences are instead 162--223\,ms. Direct is
1.36--2.83$\times$ faster than both Python-host arms at steady state.
Conversely, the compatibility arm prepares faster than both RTDL and the matched Direct
implementation. The experiment measures complete arm boundaries and has no
checker-off RTDL ablation; it therefore cannot allocate the setup difference
to checking, identity, compilation, loading, or another component. The
defensible result is lifecycle-specific arm cost, not a causal micro-cost
attribution.

Each reported ratio is the median of 18 paired block ratios, so ratios from
different columns are not algebraically composable. Confidence intervals use
10,000 fixed-seed resamples of the 18 block ratios via repeated
\texttt{randrange(18)} draws; the sorted draws at indices 249 and 9749 are the
reported bounds. Cold and prepare therefore have 18 arm observations per row,
whereas each steady worker first summarizes 64 registered executions.

The surprising Direct setup row is not explained by compilation. Frozen source
shows that all three arms consume prebuilt device artifacts: the Direct and
compatibility arms use the same matched PTX, while RTDL uses route-specific embedded
PTX. Direct invokes no NVRTC on the formal route. Each arm constructs its
context/module, program groups, pipeline, SBT, task owner, and static
allocations or GAS inside its prepare boundary, although the implementation
paths are not byte-identical. Only Direct records matching subphase timers.
Consequently, Direct's 459--472\,ms setup medians and their difference from the
compatibility arm remain descriptive; the current evidence does not identify their
cause.

\section{Programming Responsibility, Not Usability}

For the two fixed constructors and bounded triangle template, \rtdl owns
validation and generation of the
cross-role contract, physical-plan binding, executable identity, lifecycle,
and status-gated output boundary. The user does not supply raw PTX, an SBT, a
pipeline, or a native loader to the public lifecycle. The executed faults show
that these are real responsibilities rather than a line-count rubric.

No third party has authored an \rtdl application. We have no human usability
study, developer-time measurement, or functionally matched productivity
baseline. We therefore make no claim that \rtdl is easier, shorter, or more
productive than CUDA/OptiX, \pyoptix, or \owl. The measured contribution is
responsibility transfer and pre-launch rejection of declared inconsistencies.
Learnability and authoring efficiency remain open empirical questions.

\paragraph{Coverage of the taxonomy.}
The four responsibilities are a problem-scoped organization, not a claim that
every RT tool is a direct competitor. HLSL/DXR and SPIR-V/Vulkan remain in the
interface class. Renderer-domain languages such as OSL and MDL define shading
or material computation inside renderer-owned protocols
~\cite{openShadingLanguageDocs,nvidiaMDLDocs}, placing them primarily in
interface/construction rather than repurposed mapping or protocol admission.
Embree and HIP RT expose ray-traversal kernels or libraries
~\cite{embreeDocs,hiprtDocs} and likewise fall under interface/construction.
General parallel DSLs such as Taichi are mapping/generation substrates when
used to emit accelerator code~\cite{taichiDocs}. These systems are not ignored;
they are less direct comparisons because they do not document the bounded joint
admission relation evaluated here. We make no fault-survival claim for systems
we did not execute.

\paragraph{Interface systems.}
OptiX supplies a programmable ray-tracing engine~\cite{parker2010optix}.
Its payload types express per-word, per-stage access rights
~\cite{nvidiaOptix9Guide}; DXR Payload Access Qualifiers provide per-field
access contracts and require each declared read stage to follow a write and
each declared write stage to precede a later read~\cite{microsoftDXRPAQ}; and
Vulkan defines matching ray-payload and hit-attribute shader interfaces
~\cite{khronosVulkanInterfaces}. These mechanisms are closer prior art than an
untyped raw-OptiX baseline and are credited explicitly. They constrain access
and, for DXR payloads, part of producer/consumer stage ordering. CP001's exact
executed mutation changes callback control semantics from
\texttt{optixIgnoreIntersection} to \texttt{optixTerminateRay}; it is not a
missing payload-stage declaration. Even when an interface fixes machine
representation and access, it need not express nominal application meaning.
CP002 sharpens this residual for attribute slot zero: the inspected OptiX
interface exposes the attribute count and numbered accessors, but no per-slot
nominal declaration. The evaluated RTDL residual otherwise
concerns nominal attribute meaning and whole host/device protocol obligations
that survive the active OptiX mechanism. We ran no DXR executable arm and make
no cross-API survival claim.
OptiX Utility spans interface and construction through typed payload/attribute
signatures, automatic packing, usage annotations, and host-side object
helpers~\cite{optixUtility}. We credit these structural and packing mechanisms;
we did not execute that library and make no claim about faults it would catch
beyond its documented mechanisms. Slang's capability system likewise validates
target, API, stage, and feature requirements~\cite{slangCapabilities}.
Exposing OptiX payload types through another construction layer would strengthen
access checking, but it would not by itself supply nominal application intent.

\paragraph{Construction systems and the OWL boundary.}
\pyoptix is a broad Python host binding~\cite{nvidiaPyOptix} and can implement
the same applications manually.
Shader Components introduced first-class modules that encapsulate shader logic
with their parameters while retaining static specialization and efficient
binding~\cite{he2017shaderComponents}. Slang showed that constrained generics,
interfaces, associated types, parameter blocks, and reflection can implement
modular and extensible shading systems without a proprietary shader
DSL~\cite{he2018slang}. Slang could compile individual \rtdl leaves, and
\rtdl claims none of these language, specialization, or parameter-binding
mechanisms.

\owl makes an essential step beyond raw OptiX: it lifts acceleration
structures, programs, pipelines, SBT construction, resources, and launch into a
managed composition layer~\cite{nvidiaOWL}. We grant it full credit for that
responsibility. At the pinned revision, however, \owl's public contract carries
structural composition facts while the application supplies device programs
and selects callback entries; it requires no explicit application-semantic
authority for nominal slot meaning, required cross-role effects, semantic
geometry projection, status-gated consumption, or an admitted identity carried
to the selected executable. Successful composition therefore establishes that
an RT object graph is legal, not that it implements the intended application
protocol.

\owl variable names and types can document local intent and enforce host-record
layout, but \owl does not require those declarations to form a cross-role
application-semantic authority. This separation does not depend on the pinned
revision's public omission of \texttt{OptixPayloadType}. Exposing native payload
access declarations would strengthen interface checking, but would not supply
the nominal authority, physical obligations, continuation policy, or
executable-selection contract considered here.

More fundamentally, the missing information cannot be recovered from
composition alone. Fix an executable and a legal \owl object graph. That same
pair can be correct for an application whose attribute-zero contract denotes a
primitive index, yet incorrect for one whose contract denotes an application
item identifier. Without a separately supplied or otherwise formalized
intended protocol, no checker confined to the executable and \owl's structural
contract can know which interpretation is required. This is not a missing
validation switch: resolving it requires adding application-semantic authority
to the abstraction.
\rtdl does not infer which intent is correct; it requires the application to
state that authority and checks a bounded implementation against it.

In our pinned executable route, each invalid realization and its nearby legal
control use the same \owl composition skeleton and slot layout for that task;
all build and launch, whereas \rtdl rejects each corresponding declared
mismatch before launch (Table~\ref{tab:residual}). \rtdl makes the additional
authority explicit in its two fixed constructors and bounded triangle template,
and checks it against re-derived implementation facts. \owl could adopt an equivalent abstraction, but doing so
would extend its responsibility from object construction to whole-protocol
admission.

\paragraph{Mapping systems.}
Dr.Jit traces and globally specializes rendering and differentiable
computation~\cite{jakob2022drjit}; LuisaRender and LuisaCompute provide a
cross-platform embedded kernel DSL, JIT compilation, and unified resource
runtime~\cite{zheng2022luisa}; and CrossRT maps portable C++ algorithms to RT
backends and software fallbacks, including megakernel and wavefront
modes~\cite{frolov2024crossrt}. These systems address construction,
specialization, and the upstream algorithm-to-backend mapping problem. \rtdl
begins after a traversal formulation is selected and checks the resulting
callback protocol. A translator or JIT could emit the additional protocol
authority or incorporate an equivalent admission pass; this paper implements
neither integration and claims no comparable optimization, differentiation, or
cross-platform translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Protocol admission and foundations.}
\rtdl is conceptually adjacent to typestate, which restricts operations by
lifecycle state~\cite{strom1986typestate}, and to interface automata and
multiparty session types, which model temporal component interfaces and global
interaction protocols~\cite{deAlfaro2001interfaceAutomata,honda2016multiparty}.
Static FFI checking and linking types address type safety and reasoning across
language boundaries~\cite{furr2005ffi,patterson2017linkingTypes}, while
GPUVerify verifies intra-kernel data races and barrier divergence
~\cite{betts2012gpuverify}. \rtdl does not contribute a general protocol type
theory, progress or type-safety theorem, verified FFI, or GPU-kernel verifier.
It instantiates a bounded admission relation over concrete host/device callback
artifacts for current fixed/template routes and empirically tests whether each
populated obligation leaf affects the decision.

\paragraph{Repurposed-RT application population.}
Repurposed-RT applications are the workload population to which these
responsibilities apply, not another class of programming system. Individual works
may use interface or construction systems, use a mapping system, or themselves
contribute the algorithm-to-RT mapping. The recent
review's 59-document bibliometric corpus includes 35 systematically analyzed
proposed solutions spanning 32 problems~\cite{meneses2026rtcoresurvey}. It
characterizes a mapping as a coordinated design of geometric data
representation, query rays, and hit-side action. After that mapping is chosen,
the surrounding application must establish its correspondence with callbacks,
payloads and attributes, host bindings, status handling, and the launched
executable unless an equivalent protocol layer is supplied. The nine systems
underlying our thirteen author-written lanes demonstrate the breadth of this
protocol-bearing setting, not prevalence: we have not established how often
published artifacts mechanically enforce these obligations or contain faults.
Our narrower result is that selected correspondence obligations can be moved
into pre-launch admission for current bounded routes; the mapping theorem and
semantic oracle remain application responsibilities.

\section{Artifact and Reproducibility}

We constructed a compact anonymous evidence projection for the paper's five
claim layers. It contains all 19 populated-leaf mutation records; the five
executed observations for preserved PyOptiX and \owl residual routes, plus the
corresponding \rtdl decisions; the
five strongest-native typed-payload observations and a source/PTX-identical negative
control; the capability and transfer ceilings; SQL and separate Python oracle
rows; and all registered scalar timing observations from 324 same-target
three-arm workers, including the declared compatibility-arm
distribution/version/API identity and the
24/16-to-18-block registration lineage plus the withdrawn asymmetric result.
Identifying development history, machine paths, process identifiers,
endpoints, and review records are excluded by construction.

A standard-library-only verifier rehashes every manifested payload, checks and
recounts the preserved 19-row liveness projection and the $3+1+1$
PyOptiX/\owl residual classification, checks the preserved five-row
strongest-native projection and live negative control against frozen values,
checks the preserved SQL/Python equality projection, and recomputes all 18
reported comparison rows, each from 18 paired-block ratios, including the
fixed-seed bootstrap intervals and registered timing count. The performance
recount starts from 7,128
scalar observations rather than from Table~\ref{tab:perf}. This supports
numerical and claim-boundary review without access to our internal history.

The compact projection is not itself a GPU re-execution package: its mutation
and residual records are preserved execution observations, whereas the
verifier independently recomputes their stated counts and invariants. A full
GPU rerun additionally requires an OptiX SDK, compatible NVIDIA driver and
GPU, the pinned residual-route sources, the source for the compatibility arm,
and the executable build inputs. We do
not equate a successful offline recount with independent reproduction of the
hardware executions.

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}

The three most consequential evidence gaps are ordered rather than presented
as a defensive checklist. First, arbitrary verified Callback IR cannot be
authorized for GPU execution: caller source reaches GPU only inside one bounded
triangle template, while new physical or role/continuation shapes still require
core/backend changes. The falsifying next test is a new protocol shape
instantiated after a generic core freeze. Second, the five executed faults are
constructed, so their prevalence in published third-party artifacts is
unknown; the next test is a preselected, complete-denominator field corpus.
Third, every application was authored by the project, so external authoring and
learnability are unknown; the next test is a frozen-interface external-author
case series. These gaps respectively limit mechanism generality, real-world
importance, and usability evidence.

\paragraph{Generality.}
There are two fixed public constructors. Current routes instantiate two of six
OptiX build-input enum kinds (two of four leaf primitives). This measures kind
presence, not category completeness. One bounded triangle template accepts
user source. Semantic protocol shapes form an open set. The thirteen-lane
cohort is project-authored. A new geometry or callback topology currently
requires shared-core and native-backend changes. The SQL reuse case was selected
after a failed transfer. Prospective unbiased new-shape exams and third-party
authors are both zero. These facts
limit external validity and are stated in the abstract rather than deferred to
artifact documentation.

\paragraph{Mechanism scope.}
The 19-leaf sweep covers populated leaves in two accepted task contracts. It
does not prove that the vocabulary is complete or that every future leaf is
decision-bearing. The five categories are related; we do not infer five
independent theoretical contributions from five rejection rows.

\paragraph{Oracles.}
Exact task oracles detect all five registered faults on exposing inputs. \rtdl
does not prove arbitrary output correctness. Its advantage is rejecting the
declared mismatch before launch and without relying on which inputs were
sampled.

\paragraph{Performance.}
The current matched result uses two authored tasks and one RTX 4000 Ada. The cold
regime starts after Python import/runtime preload and is not full-process cold.
Preparation fails the registered margin. The baseline is \pyoptixcompat, not a
stock/current OptiX-9.1-API arm. Same-target Direct rows using current source
are descriptive and have no performance gate.
There is no \owl timing arm, checker-off preparation ablation, second target,
or third task.

\paragraph{Usability.}
The public lifecycle is executable but has not been used by an external human
author. We make no ease or productivity claim.

\section{Conclusion}

Existing RT systems principally validate interfaces, construct pipelines, or
map algorithms to backends. \rtdl studies a fourth, bounded responsibility:
admitting a repurposed RT program as one declared bounded whole protocol rather
than as isolated shaders. It admits declarations, physical plans, executable identity,
and lifecycle obligations as one unit. For two accepted
contracts, all 19 populated leaves are decision-bearing under single-leaf
mutation with projection bytes fixed: 7 role/effect, 1 semantic-ABI, 7
physical-binding, 2 continuation/completeness, and 2 executable-identity
leaves. This is contract liveness evidence, not a soundness theorem or proof
that the obligation vocabulary is complete.

Under active native OptiX typed-payload semantics exposed through a disclosed
two-line repaired PyOptiX FFI, all five preregistered fault realizations reached
launch after a source/PTX-identical negative established that native access checking
was active. A separate pinned \owl arm also built and launched all five. The
scientific result is deliberately not ``five full guarantees'': it is three
full wrong-output residuals, one partial status-gated
continuation/completeness residual, and checked-executable binding support.
\rtdl rejected the corresponding declared inconsistencies before launch.

The implementation remains bounded to two fixed constructors, one restricted
triangle authoring template, two physical geometry kinds, and project-authored
ports. Against the pinned \texttt{otk-pyoptix}~9.1.0
compatibility arm described above, steady RTDL/PyOptiX-compat passed the
registered 1.05 noninferiority gate
on two tasks, with median differences of $-17\,\mu$s and $+1.22\,\mu$s;
post-import deployment-cold and prepare instead showed 162--223\,ms setup
deltas. The Direct arm is descriptive. Prospective transfer, third-party
authorship, usability, arbitrary Callback-IR execution, and cross-API survival
remain unestablished.
