## 5. Reviewer attacks still open

1. **Novelty/sufficiency:** a reviewer may view the work as a disciplined API
   around topology-specific implementations rather than a sufficiently general
   compiler contribution. The paper's defense is the result-obligation-to-
   admission/lowering/binding/publication connection, not generic lowering.
2. **TCB weight:** generated Numba leaves coexist with trusted wrappers and
   dedicated native routes. A reviewer may conclude that too much physical
   behavior remains trusted. The paper discloses rather than hides this cost.
3. **Evaluation breadth:** the strongest successor evidence is five units on
   one A4500, and only one prepared row is multi-second. The paper makes a
   tested-route cost claim only; it cannot claim broad performance.
4. **Usability:** no independent developer authored a program or measured
   effort. The paper explicitly declines an easier-authoring claim.
5. **Fairness judgment:** successor pairs share inputs, outputs, preprocessing
   boundary, endpoint definitions, and paired process structure, but physical
   routes differ by application. Independent review must judge whether each
   exact row remains a meaningful comparison.

These are not closed by author assertion. They are the required focus of both
independent R7 reviews.
